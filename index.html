A digital design featuring a prosthetic arm displayed in separated components against a dark background. The composition uses blue and purple lighting alongside explanatory text to demonstrate design principles including emphasis, contrast, proximity, alignment, balance, and color.

Course Goals


1. Consider the role of rhetoric, technology, and ethics in acts of making.

Throughout these designs, rhetoric, technology, and ethics served as interconnected components of the design process. The UX Design Study illustrates how these considerations informed the research and revision of three personal logo concepts. Rhetoric shaped the testing script through questions designed to clearly communicate prompts and elicit meaningful feedback. Technology enabled the creation of presentation materials and the revision of logo designs in response to participant feedback. Ethics informed the study by ensuring participants understood its purpose, their role in the evaluation process, and the voluntary nature of their participation. Together, these considerations supported informed design decisions and the refinement of the final logo.

2. Leverage Design Principles.

Throughout these designs, design principles served as essential tools for communicating information and guiding audience attention. The "Good Design" project illustrates how contrast, emphasis, balance, and proximity informed the overall composition. A dark background contrasts with the illuminated prosthetic arm, creating a focal point that directs attention to the central subject. The arrangement of the prosthetic components establishes balance and reinforces relationships between individual parts. These same principles appear throughout my portfolio, where contrast establishes hierarchy, emphasis highlights key information, and balance improves organization. Together, these principles strengthen visual communication and audience understanding.

3. Engage with others in a workshop environment.

Collaboration served as an important component of the design process. The UX Design Study provides a strong example of how engagement with others strengthened the development of my user testing methodology. While constructing the testing script, I struggled to determine how visceral, behavioral, and reflective questions should be phrased to collect meaningful feedback. Engaging with my peers helped me refine the wording and organization of these questions, improving both participant comprehension and the effectiveness of the study. By incorporating feedback during the planning stage, I strengthened the research process and improved the quality of the information collected from participants.



4. Practice revision within and across media forms.

Revision played an important role throughout these designs, allowing visual and written elements to evolve beyond their initial forms. The Good Design project serves as a reminder of how revision strengthened both the presentation and communication of design principles. After completing the initial composition, I revisited the design and refined the prosthetic arm imagery by cropping and repositioning individual components to improve visual emphasis and balance. I also added a source section to document the origin of the image and strengthen the project's credibility. These revisions improved both the visual organization of the design and the communication of information to its audience.

5. Learn to present your work.

Presenting work played an important role throughout these designs by allowing me to communicate ideas and gather feedback from different audiences. The logo design project expresses how presenting work can improve both the design process and final outcome. I first presented the three logo concepts to my small group and used their reactions to evaluate the effectiveness of each design. I later presented the logos to the entire class, where I explained the technological influences behind the designs and their connection to my identity and professional interests. Feedback from both presentations informed design decisions, including the selection of background colors and the refinement of the final logo concept.

Two-page Spread

Two-page magazine spread centered on the question, “What if aging were a disease?” Large typography spelling “DISEASE” dominates the layout alongside a medical-style illustration combining anatomical imagery and a wheelchair. Article text surrounds the visuals to emphasize the publication’s central theme.

Infographic

Infographic titled “CRISPR: Rewriting Life’s Code.” A large central diagram shows the CRISPR-Cas9 protein interacting with DNA strands, with labeled sections identifying DNA modification components. Supporting sections discuss medical benefits, ethical concerns, future applications, and a highlighted 96% clinical trial success statistic.

Advertisement

Advertisement for the fictional Bugatti Altair vehicle featuring a large blue luxury car and an inset image of the vehicle’s interior. Promotional headlines and branding emphasize craftsmanship, performance, and luxury, while the car remains the dominant visual focus.

Accessible Design

Infographic with a dark background, a wearable medical device icon, and a prosthetic limb illustration. Five sections outline biomedical engineering principles focused on accessibility, safety, usability, and patient-centered design. Large headings and structured text guide readers through each principle.

Course Goals

  1. Consider rhetoric, technology, and ethics

    Throughout these projects, rhetoric, technology, and ethics served as perpetuators of design intent. The Bugatti Altair advertisement illustrates how these elements worked together to support a specific communication goal. Rhetoric shaped the advertisement's persuasive appeal through luxury-focused imagery, branding, and promotional copy designed to position the fictional vehicle as an aspirational product. Technology enabled the realization of that vision through digital design tools, including removing image backgrounds, repositioning vehicle imagery, and compositing visual elements to create a cohesive luxury aesthetic that supported the advertisement's branding and message. Ethics informed decisions regarding audience influence and responsible advertising practices, requiring consideration of how visual and textual elements guide consumer perception. Rather than functioning as separate considerations, rhetoric, technology, and ethics reinforced one another to communicate a cohesive message and strengthen the effectiveness of the final design.

  2. Leverage design principles

    Throughout these projects, design principles served as essential tools for communicating information effectively. The CRISPR infographic demonstrates how hierarchy, spacing, balance, and proximity were used to organize complex scientific content into a format that was approachable for a general audience. Careful consideration was given to the placement of the CRISPR-Cas9 diagram, supporting statistics, and explanatory sections so readers could navigate the information in a logical sequence and focus on key concepts without becoming overwhelmed. By leveraging these principles, the design improved clarity, supported audience comprehension, and strengthened engagement with the topic.

  3. Engage in a workshop environment

    Throughout the development of the accessibility-focused design project, engagement in a workshop environment played a significant role in refining my approach. Feedback from the professor encouraged me to remove text outlines that reduced readability and accessibility, while reviewing my peers' work exposed me to additional considerations for audience needs. Observing a design that used decorative typography with potential readability challenges prompted me to think more critically about how users interact with written content. These observations led me to reevaluate text sizing, alignment, spacing, and overall layout decisions to better support individuals requiring accessibility accommodations. By incorporating both critique and peer observations into the revision process, I improved the clarity and usability of the project while strengthening my understanding of accessibility-centered design and audience-focused communication.

  4. Practice revision across media

    Throughout the development of the Bugatti Altair advertisement, revision played a significant role in refining how the design communicated its intended message. Early versions focused primarily on the vehicle itself, but later revisions introduced a branded header ribbon featuring the Bugatti emblem to place greater emphasis on company identity and strengthen brand recognition. I also added a gradient overlay that enhanced the metallic appearance of the vehicle and better aligned the imagery with the luxury aesthetic conveyed through the advertisement's typography, branding, and promotional copy. Through these revisions, I refined the relationship between branding, text, and visual elements to create a more cohesive and persuasive design.

  5. Learn to present your work

    Presenting my two-page editorial magazine spread allowed me to discuss both the visual and conceptual aspects of the original design. While presenting to the class, I focused on the article's central question, “What if aging were a disease?”, because it explored a relevant and thought-provoking concept. Although the project involved recreating an existing publication layout, I explained how the placement and scale of the word “DISEASE” interacted with the accompanying imagery to create a strong visual focal point and reinforce the article's message. Preserving the impact of these elements while exploring typography, spacing, and layout relationships became a central focus of my work. Presenting the project strengthened my ability to analyze design choices and communicate how typography and composition influence the effectiveness of a visual message.
